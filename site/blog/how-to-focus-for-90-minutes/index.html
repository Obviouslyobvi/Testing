How to Focus for 90 Minutes Straight (Even If You Get Distracted Easily)

Ninety minutes is long enough to finish something real and short enough that your brain can see the finish line. The trick is not willpower. It's structure. Here is the plan.

The Launch (5 minutes)

  • Put your phone in another room. Not face down. Another room.
  • Close every tab you don't need for this one task.
  • Get water.
  • Write one sentence: "This session, I will..."
  • Start a timer.

Sprint 1 (about 25 minutes): the smallest start

Do the tiniest version of beginning. Outline, open the file, write the ugly first draft. Starting is the hardest part, so make it almost too easy.

Break (5 minutes): move

Stand up, stretch, walk to the window. Your phone stays where it is.

Sprint 2 (about 25 minutes): the middle chunk

This is where most of the real work gets done. Name the exact chunk before you start.

Break (5 minutes)

Same rules as before.

Sprint 3 (15 to 20 minutes): the finish-line push

Wrap it up. Good enough and done beats perfect and stuck.

Shutdown (10 minutes)

Write what got done. Park every loose end on a "Next Time" list. Write the very first step for your next session so tomorrow starts in seconds. Then take a small reward you actually enjoy.

Only have 45 minutes?

Shrink the sprints proportionally. Keep the launch and the shutdown no matter what. They are what make the session stick.

When distractions hit

Decide in advance. Write "If I reach for my phone, then I stand up, take one breath, and write the urge down instead." Keep a scrap of paper called the Parking Lot next to you. Every stray thought goes there until shutdown.
