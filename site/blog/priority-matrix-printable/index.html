The Priority Matrix That Accounts for Your Energy (Not Just Deadlines)

The classic urgent-versus-important grid (often called the Eisenhower Matrix) is a great idea with one blind spot: it assumes you have the same energy all day. You don't. A task that is important and urgent can still be the wrong pick if you're running on fumes.

Add a third score: energy fit

For every task on your list, give three scores:

  • Impact (1 to 5): How much does finishing this move things forward?
  • Urgency (1 to 5): Is it due soon, or is someone waiting?
  • Energy fit (Good, OK, Poor): Does the task match how you feel right now?

How to judge energy fit

  • Energy 4 to 5: deep, creative, or hard-thinking work.
  • Energy 3: steady work such as editing, planning, and prepared calls.
  • Energy 1 to 2: admin, email, filing, errands, easy checklists.

Pick the winner

Add impact and urgency. Among the tasks marked Good or OK for energy, the highest total wins. That's your ONE task for the next 90 minutes. Pick a backup, too.

What if the urgent task is a poor energy fit?

Sometimes the deadline wins anyway. When that happens, break the task into mechanical steps (a checklist you can follow without much thinking) so low energy doesn't stop you.
