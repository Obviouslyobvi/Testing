How to Do a Brain Dump That Actually Helps (Free Method)

A brain dump is simple: you write down everything on your mind so your brain stops trying to hold it all. The problem is that most people stop there. They end up with a giant list, feel worse, and close the notebook. Here is how to do one that ends with you actually working.

Step 1: Set a 5-minute timer and write everything

Tasks, worries, errands, half-ideas, the email you keep forgetting. Do not sort, do not judge, do not rewrite. One item per line. If you stall, ask yourself: what's nagging me about work, home, money, and people?

Step 2: Mark anything with a clock on it

Circle every item that is due in the next 48 hours or that someone is waiting on. These are your real deadlines. Everything else can wait at least one session.

Step 3: Check your energy honestly

Rate yourself 1 to 5. Low energy days are perfect for admin, cleanup, and quick replies. High energy days are for writing, planning, and hard thinking. Matching the task to your energy is the single biggest reason a session works or fails.

Step 4: Pick ONE task

Score each circled item on impact (1 to 5) and urgency (1 to 5). The highest total that fits today's energy wins. Pick one backup in case you get blocked. Ignore everything else for now. It will still be on the page tomorrow.

Step 5: Make the first step tiny

"Write report" is not a first step. "Open the doc and write three bullet points" is. The smaller the start, the less your brain resists it.

Why this works

Getting tasks out of your head frees up attention, and choosing just one removes the "what should I do first" loop that burns so much time. Writing the plan down also makes it easier to come back after a distraction.
