## 2. Rick in one paragraph

Rick is a lifelong lender and real estate guy who sees himself as a problem solver first. He writes to attorneys the way he'd talk to a friend over coffee. He tells real cases with first names and odd concrete details, says what the loan costs without flinching, reminds you he's not a lawyer, and closes by asking how he can help and giving his phone number. His humor is folksy and a bit corny, and he never sounds like he's selling.

## 3. What makes it Rick

### 3a. He talks to the attorney, and the attorney is his customer
- "You, the Attorney, are our customer." (Oct 2017, Rick's Rants)
- "I've always considered YOU my client; your client is my borrower. Executors and Administrators come and go, however the relationship with YOU ought to last a lifetime for both of us." (May 2019, Rick's Rants)
- "If there's one thing that I've noticed about the members of the trust and estates legal community, it's that they are some of the nicest people I'd ever expect to meet and serve." (Jan 2018)
- "As you have witnessed..." (email to Gene)

### 3b. He opens plainly, with no hook tricks
- "This month I want to talk about creditors. Especially frustrating creditors." (May/June 2018)
- "If you're like me, you are looking back on the past year..." (Dec 2017, Rants)
- "How many times have you had conversations with clients about uncomfortable tasks..." (Apr 2018)
- "You may have clients who have a time crunch where there's a pending foreclosure or other drama." (Feb 2018)
- "In 1978, I attended a very interesting talk about California property taxes, given by Howard Jarvis..." (Mar 2018)

### 3c. He tells real cases with first names and strange, specific details
- Barb: "with all the books, magazines, and unopened boxes of QVC merchandise, her home was actually shrinking" and her trust documents were "in an envelope, in a box, under many piles of junk, in the corner of an inaccessible room" (Jan 2018)
- Esther "left the Midwest cold to move into her aunt's Glendora home" and ended up relocating "to an even warmer climate." (Feb 2018)
- Bill and Barbara "married late in life. He owned a modest home but never added Barbara on the title." (Oct 2017)
- A red-tagged house "coincidentally... it was a fireman who bought the property, as is." (Dec 2017)
- Cases usually start with "In one case," "In a recent case," "Some months ago, we had a case," or "Earlier this year we had a case."
- He admits when a case was hard: "Those situations can be hard to resolve." (Nov 2017)

### 3d. Homespun sayings and metaphors
- "We don't rent hammers but we do have tools. You should, too." (Jan 2018)
- "You don't want to put a spot bandage on a bleeding artery." (Mar 2018)
- "You Can't Eat Equity, But It Can Feed You for a Long Time" (Nov 2017 headline)
- "pizza-by-the-slice" for fractional ownership (Nov 2017, Apr 2018)
- "left holding the bag" (Dec 2017); "past the five-yard line" (Feb 2018)
- The Boy Scouts helping "little old ladies cross the street," and "she still needs to get home." (Feb 2018)
- "a Rolodex (remember those?)" and "a grad from the University of Hard Knocks" (Oct 2017)

### 3e. His humor is dry and self-deprecating, and sometimes goes for the pun
- "I didn't really get into the business to be making loans to people whose homes required that they'd be wearing moon suits." (Dec 2017)
- "a lifetime of practical knowledge of tenants, toilets, moms, madams and mooches. You can't buy this kind of entertainment." (Aug 2017, Rants)
- The podcast's "no pitching/ no bitching" policy (Apr 2018, May/June 2018)
- "unmarketable (can you say hazmat?)" (May 2019)
- "80% of the problems that you tell me about... is that it bothers you! ... I'll bet they don't 'bother' teaching this in law school." (May 2019)
- Alliterative headlines: "Proper Possession Planning Prevents Pitfalls" (Aug 2017), "Crusty Creditors" (May/June 2018), "It Ain't Like It's 2008!" (Dec 2017)
- "seasoned curmudgeons like myself" (Apr 2018)

### 3f. He's open about the price
This is one of his biggest trust-builders, so keep it.
- "Of course the interest rate is higher, and isn't going to be even close to bank rates." (Dec 2017)
- "Granted it's more expensive, but it is a good alternative to either a partition action, or the appointment of a receiver." (Nov 2017)
- "Borrowers usually have to pay a premium for this kind of financing." (Mar 2018)
- "loans obtained during administration are offered at a modest premium. It's not permanent financing." (Sept 2025)

### 3g. Things he says over and over
Use these as recurring phrases. Just don't cram them in.
- **Problem solver first, lender second:** "Loans are our secondary, not primary, business." (Jan 2018); "My job is not just to make loans." (Feb 2018); "I'm in the problem-solving business, not the cheap loan business." (May 2019); "My first aim is to solve people problems; lending money is secondary to that." (Apr 2018)
- **Talk to the client directly:** he makes this point in Jan, Feb, Mar and Apr 2018. For example, "If I insist on talking to the client, now you'll understand why." (Feb 2018)
- **A loan is "a process, not an event."** (Feb 2018)
- **"Who, not how."** (Apr 2018); also "the right 'who'" (May 2019)
- **"Begin with the end in mind."** (Jan 2018); "Clients need to see themselves six months or more in the future. Give them a vision and then provide them a path." (Feb 2018)
- **Terms he uses a lot:** "cash poor estate or trust," "property puzzles," "fiduciary mortgage," "liquidity," "handholding," "messy," "drama," "equity," "interlopers"
- **Stock phrases:** "It's my great pleasure." "I'm always happy to help." "by all means." "Truth-be-told." "In truth." "Granted,"

### 3h. What annoys him
- Rate shoppers: "Most people who call are only interested in rates... for a tricky problem... that seems pretty shortsighted." (Apr 2018); "A surprising number of attorneys still call looking for rates and terms." (May 2019)
- Hard-money brokers and commodity lenders: "hard-money brokers, ignorant of the nuances..." (Aug 2017); "a list of commodity lenders. Many of these lenders appeared on the scene after the last Great Recession" (Oct 2017); "'mortgage mill' lenders" (May/June 2018)
- Big banks and servicers: "faceless bank bureaucrats, those hiding behind desks, phones and systems" (May/June 2018)
- Title and escrow overcomplicating things: "if you're not vigilant, it's likely they will find a way to make your simple, elegant solution more complicated." (Oct 2017)
- Sly reverse-mortgage lenders: "one of the hidden gotchas that sly reverse-mortgage lenders don't tell you about." (Nov 2018)

### 3i. He's modest about his expertise
- "Because I am a layperson I'm certainly not qualified to give legal advice." (Nov 2017)
- "you are reminded that I am a layperson, not an attorney." (Nov 2017, Q&A)
- He calls himself a "Layperson Team Member." (Oct 2017)

### 3j. How he closes
He ends softly: a question or an invitation, then the phone number, and no pressure.
- "This is where you call me at (800) 779-2552. How can I help you and your client?" (Oct 2017)
- "Need help to find the catalyst and create the path? Let's talk about solutions before we worry about payments." (Jan 2018)
- "Together we can solve a lot of problems." (Feb 2018)
- "Want to explore solutions? Call me directly at (800) 779-2552." (Nov 2018)
- "Want to discuss creditor payment strategies? Please call me directly" (May 2019)
- "We're happy to be of assistance whether or not there's a transaction." (Dec 2017)
- Email sign-off: "Stay Liquid! ~Rick" and "Bring your messiest and most complicated financing." (email to Gene)

## 4. How his sentences work

- **They sound like him talking.** Long, loose sentences sit next to short ones. "I'm a real estate and lender guy; it's my 40th year. I think I know the business." (Mar 2018)
- **He asks rhetorical questions and then answers them.** "I love these activities. Don't you?" (Dec 2017); "But what is the catalyst?" (Jan 2018)
- **He uses lots of parentheses.** "(or re-connecting)", "(remember those?)", "(can you say hazmat?)", "(from the outside)"
- **He loves lists.** The 18-item task list (Apr 2018), the four roles (Oct 2017), and bulleted client types (Aug 2017)
- **He uses caps for emphasis, but rarely.** "YOU" (May 2019, email); "KEEP" (email)
- **He uses exclamation points mostly in Rants and emails, and not in case stories.** "My annual Gratitude Issue!" (Nov 2017); "The time to initiate a business relationship is before you need the favor!" (Oct 2017); "Shameless!" (email)
- **He barely uses em dashes.** He uses hyphens with spaces instead: "unable - or at least unwilling - to take any action" (Jan 2018). Heavy em dash use shows up only in the Nov 2018 article and the Oct 2025 draft, the two pieces that sound least like him.
- **His grammar is a little rough, and that's genuine.** "glacially slowly," "most all of these cases" (email); "I turned 70th" (Sept 2025); "As a long-time, (Private) money, direct lender, investor" (Aug 2025). Don't polish every sentence smooth. On the other hand, don't add typos on purpose to printed copy.

## 5. The sections

**Main article.** First person. Starts with a plain setup, then one or two real cases, then practical advice, then the soft CTA.

**Fiduciary Q&A.** It ran in every printed issue from 2017 to 2019 and was dropped in the 2025 drafts. It's a reader-style question with a plain, technical answer, sometimes with "Suppose..." or "Let's assume..." hypotheticals (Jan 2018).

**Rick's Rants.** Personal and opinionated. Topics he has covered:
- His business philosophy (Oct 2017, Jan 2018, May 2019)
- Industry and legal news: SB 2 recording fees (Feb 2018), federal timelines on reverse-mortgage foreclosures (Apr 2018)
- Charity: The Midnight Mission for about 30 years (Nov 2017, Nov 2018)
- His life: his parents Don (died at 56) and Anna (lived to 89) (Nov 2017); "My Dad was an orphaned teenager from Chicago. My Mom, the youngest child of farmers" (Mar 2018); selling his San Diego County ranch (Dec 2017); the Alisal Ranch conference and his mastermind groups (Apr 2018, Mar 2018); turning 70 in July 2025 (Sept 2025)
- The Gordian Knot Show podcast (Dec 2017, Apr 2018, May/June 2018)
- Market commentary (Aug and Sept 2025)

**Curmudgeon Corner.** From 2017 to 2019 this was **always a licensed cartoon** about wills and inheritance. The captions included "To my favorite waiter I leave the usual 20 percent." (Aug 2017), "I can't believe she left everything to that stupid parakeet!" (Oct 2017) and "...AND TO MY CAT, WILHELM, I LEAVE MY WIFES BEST COUCH!" (Dec 2017). The 2025 drafts switched to quotes with a named source (George Burns, Red Green). Either works. Keep it about wills, death, old age or lawyers.

## 7. Fixes to your saved voice notes

1. **"Dry, not corny" is too strict.** He does puns and alliteration ("no pitching/ no bitching", "moms, madams and mooches", "Proper Possession Planning Prevents Pitfalls"). A better rule: folksy and a bit corny, never slick.
2. **"Curmudgeon Corner is a 1-2 sentence closing joke."** Historically it was a cartoon, so a quote or a cartoon caption both fit.
3. **"Not hypotheticals."** He does use "Suppose two heirs..." and "Let's assume that a house... doesn't care" (Jan 2018 Q&A). What he avoids is "Picture this" and "Imagine." Plain "Suppose" is fine.
4. **"Never explain their own job."** That holds for law. He does explain lending and real estate mechanics to attorneys ("REO, meaning real estate-owned", "What is a Performance Trust Deed?").
5. **Add: he's open about price.** He always says what the loan costs.
6. **Add: the Fiduciary Q&A section.** It ran in all 10 printed issues.
7. **The anchor quote in your notes ("I had one case in Northern California...") doesn't appear in any of these files.** I couldn't check it against these sources.

## 8. Quick test before a draft goes to Rick

- [ ] Does it talk to the attorney as "you"?
- [ ] Is there at least one real case, anonymized, with a concrete detail? (If you don't have one, put [NEED: case from Rick] rather than making one up.)
- [ ] Does it say what the loan costs, or what its limits are?
- [ ] Does it mention "problem solver first" or "talk to the client directly" somewhere?
- [ ] Does it end with a question and the phone number, with no pressure?
- [ ] Is it free of slogans, neat "not X, Y" lines, "game changer", "picture this", and em dashes?
- [ ] Is there at least one folksy line or bit of dry humor?
- [ ] Read it out loud. Does it sound like a 70-year-old lender talking across a table, not a marketer?
